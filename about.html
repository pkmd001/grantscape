GrantScape — Help & About

The NIH funding landscape for grant strategy.

← Back to the app

GrantScape integrates three public NIH data sources into one browser tool so investigators can see what NIH funds, where funding is growing, and which institutes and study sections fund a given research area. Everything runs locally in your browser; nothing is uploaded. On the hosted site, the three tabs load real NIH data automatically when you open the page.

Funding Map

What NIH funds by research category (RCDC), and which areas are rising.

  • From/To FY — the fiscal-year window for growth. (FY2025 is an NIH estimate; end at FY2024 for full-actual figures.)
  • Momentum — Absolute $ growth is a category's compound annual growth. Relative to portfolio subtracts the median category's growth, isolating fields rising faster than NIH overall (removing the macro-budget trend).
  • Min funding — hides small-base categories whose percentages swing wildly on trivial dollar amounts (default ≥$50M).
  • Search or click a category to see its funding trajectory.

Concept Landscape

For a research concept, which institutes and study sections fund it.

  • Choose a bundled concept (transplantation, regenerative medicine) or load your own CSVs.
  • Generate new concepts with scripts/pull_concept_landscape.py or the Colab notebook, which support keyword combinations (AND / OR / Boolean).
RePORTER contains funded projects only (no application denominator), so this tab shows where funded work lands, not success rates.

Institute Odds

Success rate and funding by Institute/Center (NIH Data Book). Pick a fiscal year to see one row per IC, sorted by success rate. These are new (Type 1) RPG success rates; the primary column is investigator-initiated (untargeted). Rates are administrative and IC-level — not specific to your topic within an institute.

Putting it together

The three tabs answer a single strategic question: for my science, which institutes and study sections fund it, is that area growing, and what are the odds there? For example, transplantation research concentrates at NCI (lower current odds), whereas regenerative-medicine research concentrates at NIGMS (higher current odds) — so where a field is reviewed can matter as much as the science.

Data sources & limitations

RCDC categorical spending and success rates: report.nih.gov; funded projects: NIH RePORTER. RCDC categories overlap and NIH does not budget by category; study-section success rates are not published anywhere. GrantScape is descriptive — a map to inform, not determine, submission strategy.
